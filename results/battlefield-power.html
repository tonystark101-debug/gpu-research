The Jetson figure combines its board's measured power under AI load (18–22 W, of which we take 50–70% for the GPU, split 8 ways) with the A100 core's work per tick from our yardstick. The comparison flatters us: our core sits on a newer-class process and leaves out the shared cache and memory controllers.

What to change in our own core, biggest first

Each lever comes straight from these measurements.

  1. Clock gating: switch off the clock to idle units. 80–94% of the power at realistic activity is idle storage being clocked.
  2. Register files in dense memory, not flip-flops. The 1.81 million flip-flops are 34% of the logic area and most of the clock load.
  3. A smaller general maths unit. It is 57% of the logic and almost idle in AI jobs.
  4. A deeper copy engine. 2.9× faster on realistic memory, for about 1% more storage cells.
